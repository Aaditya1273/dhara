{# ================= A. THE PROBLEM ================= #}
The problem

A cleaning record can't see inside the tank.

Registers say when someone cleaned. They don't say how warm the water is now, whether any chlorine is left, who checked the evidence, or what a laboratory found. In a hot summer, those are the questions that matter.

CLEANED

  • How warm is the water today?Rooftop tanks heat up; nobody measured.
  • Is any free chlorine left?No reading, no time, no method.
  • Did anyone check the evidence?Photos sit in a phone gallery.
  • What did the lab report, and when?The PDF is in an email thread.

{# ================= D. TWO LANES ================= #}
Two lanes of evidence

Connected by accountability. Never merged.

A completed repair or a clean photograph is not proof of potable water. DHARA keeps maintenance evidence and water-quality observations apart, and lets both end in one audit trail.

Maintenance evidenceWhat work was done, and who checked it
1

Work submitted

Claimed work date and the server's own submission time, kept separately.

2

Photos and documents

Stored privately, each with a SHA-256 fingerprint.

3

Warden decision

Accept, reject or ask for more, with a reason. Never by the submitter.

4

Correction, not edit

Reviewed events stay unchanged; a correction is a new event.

The lanes never merge

Water-quality observationsWhat was measured, how and by whom
1

Field reading

Temperature or free chlorine with method, time and source: a person or a probe.

2

Strip photo

Optional evidence image with its own digest.

3

Laboratory report

Sample date and parameters exactly as the report states.

4

Institution review

Marked reviewed by a warden. Still not independent verification.

Both lanes end in one audit trail

Every entry records who acted, what changed and the server time. An ordinary log without edit screens, not a tamper-proof ledger.

{# ================= E. WORKFLOW (sticky story) ================= #}
From observation to resolution

Six steps. Each one leaves a trace.

Every step produces something a warden, an auditor or a student can check later.

Step 1 of 6: Measure

  1. 1

    Measure

    A technician records tank-water temperature or free chlorine from a phone, or an in-tank probe sends readings through AWS IoT Core.

    probe reading, method, source and time
  2. 2

    Prioritise

    Rules compare reading age and values with your thresholds and the weather forecast, and explain every reason.

    {% badge 'priority' 'high' %} with the rules that fired
  3. 3

    Record evidence

    Maintenance is submitted with before-and-after photos and documents. Files are validated on the server and fingerprinted.

    {% badge 'evidence' 'attached' %} with SHA-256 digests
  4. 4

    Review

    A warden who did not submit the work accepts it, rejects it or asks for more evidence, with a reason.

    {% badge 'review' 'pending' %} then {% badge 'review' 'accepted' %}
  5. 5

    Follow up

    Unmet schedules, missing reports and reviewer requests open follow-ups for the responsible person.

    {% badge 'task' 'open' %} assigned, with a due date
  6. 6

    Verify resolution

    A task closes only when someone records what was done. The public QR page shows the outcome without private details.

    {% badge 'task' 'resolved' %} with notes and a name
{# ================= F. TRUST ================= #}
Trust and transparency

Clear about what it knows, and what it doesn't.

Recorded

  • What was measured, and the method
  • When it was measured and when entered
  • Who submitted it, or which probe
  • Which lab documents exist

Reviewed by

  • Wardens of that institution only
  • Never the person who submitted
  • With a reason for rejections
  • Roles that apply per institution

Traceable

  • SHA-256 for every evidence file
  • Re-check a digest at any time
  • Corrections as new events
  • An activity log of every action

Not claimed

  • That water is safe to drink
  • That a photo is genuine
  • That a forecast is a measurement
  • That the log is tamper-proof
